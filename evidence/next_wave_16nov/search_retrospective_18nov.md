## Where new evidence would change our thinking

1. **Separate task evidence from person, device and session cues.** We have cross-corpus results, including a real exact-package Scherer test. Scherer uses 19 native and 22 reconstructed channels and different tasks and people. Reserve distinct evaluation people before fitting or choosing another cross-corpus readout. Measure corrections by class and interface alongside the actual three-class decision. Keep the public P04/P10 returning checks separate. [External evidence](lunch_qualification385.md)
2. **Broaden the training signal.** §277 already tested contrastive and masked pretraining. Its first comparison used 74,617 external windows but a weak linear probe. §389 tests whether task-specific dynamics predict the next EEG sample. §391 tests transitions among local amplitude ranks. Their features differ, but both ask about local temporal dynamics. The next exploration slot should change the data or supervision axis, such as testing whether a learned WORD/SUB contrast transfers across studies, with native-channel and matched-capacity controls. Record earlier §317/352/356 comparisons before choosing that design. [Earlier coverage review](solution_space_review_17nov.md)
3. **Measure what evidence can change a decision.** §383 showed that additional correct predictions can be canceled by new mistakes. §386 found that fourfold greater training-row turnover gave almost no improvement. §390 tests whether exchanging only borderline examples preserves useful high-confidence rows. §392 tests whether an explicit-normalization decoder can identify which A6 corrections survive in the complete stack. The two A6 variants share initialization, so agreement could just conserve their shared errors. [Turnover evidence](turn386.md) · [Whole-decoder evidence](joint382.md)
4. **Budget by scientific question.** Count a smoke, controls, seeds, array shards and a collector as one question. For the next block allocate two distinct questions to exploration and two to refinements. Track actual CPU, A100 and H100 time separately. Use the same full scorer on identical rows and orders; use the original streaming path to validate promising changes. A fast replay reduces evaluation cost. It cannot repair repeated participant selection or establish a sealed gain.

## Morning review of source history and blind spots

Git history now spans the from-scratch waveform decoder (`640534e`), original A6 joint training (`b0cf5ed`), harmonic phase (`53ed18f`), cohort transport with fixed training panels (`c72ee93`) and exact-archive external memory tests (`fb0c897`, `5700ecd`). Three comparisons changed how to interpret the earlier results.

- **Session memory carries real information.** In the A6-initialized decoder, replacing the correct history with blank history cost 5.89 and 5.29 pp across two training seeds. The jointly trained model still trailed explicit A6. The unresolved engineering question is which session statistics transfer beyond the four A6 evaluation people. [Joint training](joint382.md)
- **Standalone effects can reverse with the people or the consumer.** Holding the training panel fixed left a 3.37 pp burst-duration effect difference between the two public evaluation cohorts. The corresponding difference after motor-only insertion was 0.11 pp with an interval spanning zero. In a separate comparison, random electrode pairings reproduced within-person asymmetry even better than anatomical pairs. Person-specific repeatability alone is insufficient evidence of a task mechanism. [Common panels](transport410.md) · [Anatomical control](lateral417.md)
- **External questions can be narrower and more informative.** Counting word generation and subtraction separately against their own randomized baseline allowed 26 and 29 real participants to contribute without treating fixation as a competition label. The exact candidate expressed a stronger word-specific response and the matched history interruption reduced this advantage. Subtraction remained unresolved. These task-selectivity effects govern external class decisions; the sealed ranking uses three-class cell-balanced accuracy. [Matched external windows](selectivity415.md)

The remaining release uncertainties are the private session mix, the cost of initiating fine-tuning on the actual worker, and the generality of relatively small improvements on nine external Scherer people. Morning qualification should improve those estimates. An additional model trained on the same four development people would contribute little independent release evidence.

## Pre-lunch review at 11:03 CET

Two new controls change the interpretation of learned memory. On the compact waveform model, zeroing memory at the correct age cost 3.70 pp, but replacing it with a constant source-average write gained 0.14 pp. On the stronger A6 body genuine history beat donor history by about seven points at both seeds. The measured value of knowing the session therefore depends on the representation and the control. The gain439 → gain441 → gain442 sequence moved from ten held people, to a new initialization, to a different body. The coherent augmentation effect shrank at each step. [Matched-age controls](gain441.md) · [A6 transport](gain442.md)

The more decision-relevant test sent only the session-specific logit change through the complete historical decoder. Its two deltas were +0.021 and +0.002 pp, with both intervals crossing zero. Adding 5% of the full output lowered accuracy at both seeds. A second full-system test preserved subtraction-versus-word odds and changed only the motor probability; that insertion also lowered overall accuracy. These results redirect work toward the information needed by the actual adaptation stage, or genuinely independent training populations. Reusing more seeds on the current four A6 participants would answer a narrower question. The open pre-lunch release checks address sensitivity to external electrode reconstruction, the cost of online refits, and external motor-task coverage.
