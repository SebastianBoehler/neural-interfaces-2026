# §426 Time-aligned eye activity and word-task selectivity

Aligned subtraction changed 22–24% of decisions for each archive and cohort. On the even-person reserve, cap250's word-task selectivity advantage was +5.13 pp intact and +5.00 pp after aligned subtraction. The calibrated intervention changed decisions, but the differential response between packages did not establish a time-specific ocular explanation. The intervals also admit meaningful effects.

## Measurement

Each exact archive received 60 windows per person on two pinned shuffled orders. For each run, selectivity is the word-generation minus fixation change in the probability of a WORD decision, minus the corresponding change in the probability of a SUB decision. Equal weighting across three runs gives one measure per person and order. The primary contrast compares the cap250-minus-v5.1b difference across input conditions. All 26 people previously contributed to §415 and §421. The odd and even cohorts comprise disjoint people for the intervention.

The raw recording supplies HEOG and VEOG. For each target run, two-channel least squares estimates the transfer to the 26 native EEG channels used by the original export. Calibration uses a separate run from the same participant (runs 1, 2 and 3 use runs 3, 1 and 2, respectively). Each calibration interval runs from ten to four seconds before a marker. Each six-second interval contributes 300 twenty-millisecond means, centered separately per channel and interval. The fit uses no task labels or task-period samples. Interval selection uses event times. Using run 3 to calibrate run 1 makes this a retrospective instrument.

The intervention applies the original continuous recording filters and resampling to the eye signals. Each four-second eye waveform predicts a contribution on native scalp electrodes, then the original spherical-spline mapping takes it to 41 target channels. Sixteen target channels are directly measured. The control archive receives the original exported EEG. Both experimental arms subtract the same estimated scalp waveform. One preserves its timing and the other reverses time inside each crop, preserving per-channel energy, mean and amplitude spectrum. Reversal retains any temporally symmetric component and changes the shape of the combined EEG input. Aligned subtraction can also remove cerebral or muscular activity correlated with EOG. The experiment tests the response of the frozen packages to this intervention and cannot identify the physiological origin of every removed component.
