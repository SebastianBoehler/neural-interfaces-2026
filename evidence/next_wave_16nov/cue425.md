# Sensitivity of the external archive comparison to the EEG crop

The external Scherer task cue disappears 1.25 seconds after onset. The original four-second EEG window includes the last 250 ms of the cue. Moving the window to cue offset changed cap250’s advantage over v5.1b by **+0.017 percentage points** [80% person interval −0.797,+0.813]. The interval extends beyond the preregistered ±0.5 pp equivalence margin.

| Start after cue onset | v5.1b cell-balanced accuracy | cap250 cell-balanced accuracy | cap250 minus v5.1b |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.00 s, original | 48.677% | 48.717% | +0.040 pp |
| 1.25 s, cue offset | 47.226% | 47.282% | +0.056 pp |
| 1.50 s | 46.362% | 45.777% | −0.585 pp |

Both packages became less accurate at later crop starts. The second 250 ms shift changed their difference by −0.642 pp [−1.267,−0.023]. The contrast between the first and second shifts was +0.658 pp [−0.628,+1.872]. Task evidence evolves after onset, and filtering can spread activity across the cue boundary. Neither contrast identifies a visual cause.

The worker used genuine consecutive EEG from all 2,130 trials in nine people, with complete session resets and the same two §411b orders. Reprocessing the raw recordings reproduced every original sample exactly. Shifted crops reused the original bytes on temporal overlap and appended the measured tail. Both native reset/public-control smokes and both official scorers passed. The primary comparison used jobs 3124721–23 and 3124738–45. The §425b repeat uses the other two existing delivery orders on the same people.
