# EEG methods: source-checked research, 16 November 2026

Research only. No weights were downloaded and no training, scoring, or cluster jobs were started for this note. The suggestions below are hypotheses, not registered experiments or measured PROTEUS gains. Source dates, labels, cohorts, and evaluation protocols are deliberately kept separate from our results.

DEFT's inspected CBraMod adapter file has SHA-256 `9dbb3104349eb14875e3bf9f73ed0f0d7f433d9befd5195ebbb10eee8ad07229`.

## Task and local evidence

The target is three-class motor imagery, mental subtraction, and word generation. Inputs contain 41 channels and four seconds at 120 Hz. New people and returning people both matter. The official metric averages participant × session × interface cells; development estimates also need people-nested intervals. Inference receives one session in shuffled delivery order; it may use only the current and previously delivered windows from that session. There is a 60-minute budget and no evaluation-time downloads. See `docs/STATE.md` and `OFFICIAL_CONTRACT.md`.

Existing evidence limits specific claims, not the whole solution space. On the ten-person new-person base screen, §280 CBraMod fine-tuning F1–F5 had marginals from −1.30 to −0.04 points over the shipped three; REVE fine-tuning had +0.48 [−0.94, +1.80] on the reported form, with A100 cost 7.45× the four shipped nets and a 276.8 MB checkpoint (§280/§283). The relative effects of new adaptation methods are still untested. §285 subject-adversarial FBCNet lost 3.01 points alone; this is not a test of explicit content/style factorization. §290's hand-built hierarchical core P1a and P1b lost 16.83 and 9.53 points alone; these are not tests of learned hyperbolic geometry. The corrected §281 complete-personalisation score has S3 −0.220 [−0.574, +0.140] points on new people; its real returning-person routed tail is not established. See `docs/LEDGER.md` §§280, 283, 285, 288–290 and the §281 score note.

## A. Vision encoder with a learned signal display: SeeEEG

**Source and status.** [Wang and Liu, arXiv:2611.17031](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.17031), submitted 12 November 2026: a preprint, not an established head-to-head result on PROTEUS. The authors use frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16, train display parameters and a classifier, and compare against seven frozen EEG encoders with matched head families and validation criteria. SeeEEG has its own supervised learned display; the baselines select among compatible fixed montages. A view renders one channel per row of 16-pixel tokens, either as intensity or a 1–64 Hz wavelet power map. A learned montage and filtering change the display. Five-fold cross-fitting trains each display against a head that did not see that training subject.

**Evidence and limits.** On four-class PhysioMI, balanced accuracy is 47.2% for SeeEEG versus 45.1% for its best frozen EEG baseline, REVE. On imagined speech it is 35.6% versus 29.0% for EEGPT. Both comparisons are frozen-encoder comparisons, not our metric or an evaluation against our full system. Their results average three seeds. PhysioMI uses the [EEG-FM-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17742) subject split: IDs 1–69 for training, 70–88 for validation, and 89–110 for test, with 6,210/1,734/1,803 windows reported. That gives far more training participants than our nine-person leave-one-person-out training folds. The same benchmark explicitly marks EEGPT/PhysioMI as pretraining-overlap sensitive. Its separately reported fully fine-tuned REVE reaches 61.90% on PhysioMI, illustrating that the frozen comparison does not establish superiority to fine-tuning; studies differ in processing and training, so this is not a controlled head-to-head.

**Exact transfer and compute.** A principled test would train the display and head entirely on the nine training people, use a person-disjoint inner validation selection, and evaluate the held-out person with no access to future windows. Repeat across held-out people and compare both a complete alternative and a new view inside the current complete system. The ten-person data makes five cross-fit heads and display selection high-variance. The method's per-window image has approximately 41 × 50 = 2,050 tokens after resampling 480 samples to 800 at 200 Hz, if all 41 channels are rendered and no reduction is used. A standard 224 × 224 image has 196 tokens. The ratio of attention pairs is roughly (2050/196)² ≈ 109; this is an **operation-count inference**, not a latency measurement. Multiple displays or views multiply inference work. Its 64 Hz upper scalogram range exceeds the original input's 60 Hz Nyquist limit. Exact A100 latency, memory and dynamic-image support remain unmeasured.

**Readiness and rights.** The 11-page preprint refers to supplementary details but the accessible arXiv paper does not include the supplement, exact SeeEEG code or an implementation license. The paper itself is CC BY-NC-ND; that is not a software license. The [DINOv3 model card](https://huggingface.co/timm/vit_base_patch16_dinov3.lvd1689m) provides a ViT-B safetensors file of 342,579,728 bytes according to Hugging Face's model-file API. [Meta's DINOv3 license](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3/blob/main/LICENSE.md) covers both model and code, permits distribution under its terms and requires supplying the agreement; publication acknowledgment is required. Check actual checkpoint access and competition redistribution terms before any use. No claim of low inference cost follows from the small number of trained display parameters.

## B. Language-aligned EEG: LEAF

**Source and status.** [LEAF v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24302) first appeared 29 September 2025 and was revised 31 October 2026. It is listed in [NeurIPS 2026's official paper list](https://neurips.cc/Downloads/2026). It pretrains a spectral/temporal EEG tower, then aligns it to task instructions and textual class prototypes. Its claimed best result on 12 of 16 datasets is **supervised encoder plus MLP fine-tuning**, not zero-shot text classification. The authors' direct-inference evaluation is on held-out examples of tasks included in instruction tuning; separately, their site describes Dreyer2023 and Weibo2014 as held-out datasets. The main paper reports person-disjoint splits for motor imagery and workload but a within-subject trial split for speech.

**Exact transfer.** The [released implementation](https://github.com/Jiang-Muyun/LEAF) maps named electrodes to 65 channels and accepts a four-second window resampled to 200 Hz as eight 100-sample tokens. The tower has 20.230M parameters; the instruction Q-Former adds 6.194M, excluding the text encoder. To investigate, compare a person-disjoint trained three-class head, instruction-conditioned text prototypes, and matched random-initialization/control models under the actual shuffled session stream. The current 120 Hz input cannot recover its originally absent 60–70 Hz band. Training-person labels and identities are available only within each development fit. Any language embeddings could be precomputed offline, but that is an unmeasured design change.

**Critical preprocessing difference.** The [paper's Appendix B.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24302) pools each held-out subject's trials, channels and time for robust scaling. The released [`pipeline`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiang-Muyun/LEAF/main/leaf_datasets/shared.py) likewise computes percentiles over the entire supplied batch. Reusing either procedure across target windows would consume later windows and may cross sessions. Single-window or strictly past-only normalization is possible but changes the published protocol and must be measured and guarded.

**Provenance, rights and readiness.** The paper names six pretraining datasets: Stieger2021, SEED French, SEED German, SEED sleep-deprivation, LargeSpanish and ThinkOutLoud. PROTEUS is not named; that alone does not prove participant-level non-overlap. The authors' GitHub release reports an 81,048,037-byte tower checkpoint and a 105,825,027-byte instruction checkpoint, SHA-256 `bd5c29ddc047d0765fe72cbd4ee5e95b2bba37dd8632c2bfd31185fc010163ee` and `f9ca9cabbd734d2691fa6748adf3e78056f895d4031f7edc481e7d7b6822f3fa`, respectively. The [repository license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiang-Muyun/LEAF/main/LICENSE) permits non-commercial use and redistribution with conditions; separate checkpoint licensing and the competition's intended redistribution remain to be checked. Encoder latency and peak memory on the actual worker are unknown.

## C. Process-conditioned EEG: BrainPro

**Source and status.** [BrainPro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22050) first appeared 26 September 2025; its latest listed revision is 21 October 2026. It appears in [NeurIPS's 2026 paper list](https://neurips.cc/Downloads/2026). The authors' [release](https://github.com/yi-ding-cs/BrainPro) combines an encoder shared across tasks with a motor, affective or other-process encoder. It retrieves spatial filters using electrode and region identities. Its released example accepts four-second 200 Hz windows. The benchmark has separate speech, motor imagery, and arithmetic-versus-rest tasks, not our joint task. Selecting one process branch for MI plus two cognitive labels is not obviously correct.

**Readiness.** The official checkpoint file is 224,068,414 bytes, according to the Hugging Face file API, and the model release includes [CBCR non-commercial terms](https://huggingface.co/dycs/BrainPro-V1/blob/main/LICENSE). The [training corpus configuration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yi-ding-cs/BrainPro/main/configs/pretraining_data.json) lists several motor, affective and clinical recordings but does not name PROTEUS; participant-level overlap remains unverified. Unsupported channel names are reported and discarded. A test must assert which of all 41 electrodes are retained, input units are scaled once, and the intended process branch actually reaches the classifier. Test static branch alternatives or an explicitly trained shared/multi-branch predictor on person-disjoint data; do not infer a winner from the labels alone. Speed, memory and the cost of retaining multiple branches are unmeasured.

## D. DEFT: separate task content and subject style

**Source and status.** [DEFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14497), submitted 10 October 2026 and in the [NeurIPS 2026 paper list](https://neurips.cc/Downloads/2026), trains an existing EEG backbone and an adapter that separates task content from a subject-consistent style. Its recursive Gaussian updates, within-trial temporal-variance constraint, subject-aware contrastive loss and reconstruction loss are trained using the labelled training participants. The subject ID is not needed for inference.

**Evidence and limits.** In the authors' subject-disjoint comparisons, CBraMod+DEFT improves from 46.30% to 55.79% on four-class BCIC-IV-2a (five train / two validation / two test people), and from 63.16% to 72.92% on arithmetic versus rest (26 / 5 / 5 people). A parameter-matched generic adapter reaches 48.06% and 65.97%, respectively. REVE's additional checks show +5.52 points for arithmetic and +0.61 for motor imagery. These are different data and do not estimate our gain. The content/style distinction is incomplete: on BCIC-IV-2a the subject probe is 82.22% for content and 82.52% for style. The identity signal is also useful for our returning-person gate, so indiscriminately deleting it could harm that path.

**Comparison with our actual training.** §280 tested F1–F5, three seeds and leave-one-person-out folds; one of the nine available people was the inner validation person, leaving eight fit people. F1/F2/F4 fine-tuned the backbone with different rates, F3 fine-tuned only late layers, and F5 used per-window channel z-scores instead of causal input alignment. The best marginal, F3, was −0.04 [−1.43,+1.24] points over the three-family base. CBraMod has no batch-normalization layers, so its input alignment was causal but no SessionBN was inserted. Our local Braindecode CBraMod flattens 41 × 4 × 200 token values directly into three logits: 98,403 head parameters. The [DEFT BCIC head](https://github.com/wythedee/DEFT/blob/875b95fe729c07b8b20b69c1104651863f984682/CBraMod/models/model_for_bciciv2a.py) flattens 22 × 4 × 200 values through 800- and 200-unit hidden layers, about 14.24M head parameters. Head capacity is therefore a material confound in any direct comparison. §280 pins the Braindecode-converted checkpoint at SHA-256 `a939ace9aa1e229f08391ad8bb2d197b507ae2c519a50addf087f0151b2df5c3`; DEFT expects an original-format `CBraMod.pth` and a separate implementation. Verify mapped tensors and forward parity before treating them as the same checkpoint and encoder. §285 applied gradient-reversal subject classification to FBCNet and lost 3.01 points alone; that is a negative result for a different mechanism, backbone and training recipe, not a DEFT result. A paired DEFT comparison must keep the same data, fold, reference checkpoint, normalization, inner validation, training budget and seed, and compare both our small head and a capacity-matched head. Then test whether any improvement survives all stages of the full current system. The equal-budget comparison against a complete standalone alternative remains useful too.

**Controls and reproducibility.** On BCIC-IV-2a, a matched-capacity task-only MLP scores 48.06% vs DEFT's 55.79%; these are three-seed means on fixed subject partitions. A same-loss, near-parameter-matched attention factorizer scores 43.95%; its quoted diagnostic has no accompanying seed spread. FACED sampler-only is 54.41%, versus 53.87% vanilla and 60.05% DEFT; this is a different task. These comparisons do not quantify people-resampled uncertainty. Training-only person IDs and subject-balanced sampling need guards for the eight fit people, especially at batch boundaries. The [paper's Appendix D.4–D.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14497) reports a 2.07M-parameter active CBraMod adapter. A separate representative training diagnostic reports +2.32M total trainable parameters, +90.4 MiB peak allocated GPU memory and +9.74% one-step training FLOPs. These are distinct accounting measures and are **not** inference latency. Our existing eager A100 measurement is 0.296 ms per window for plain CBraMod, not DEFT.

**Release/paper mismatch.** The paper claims subject-balanced batches ensure a positive pair for every anchor. The released [CBraMod main script](https://github.com/wythedee/DEFT/blob/875b95fe729c07b8b20b69c1104651863f984682/CBraMod/scripts/train_main.sh) does not pass `--subject_samples`; its [parser](https://github.com/wythedee/DEFT/blob/875b95fe729c07b8b20b69c1104651863f984682/CBraMod/disentangle_v7/train.py) defaults that option to zero, and the [loader](https://github.com/wythedee/DEFT/blob/875b95fe729c07b8b20b69c1104651863f984682/CBraMod/disentangle_v7/data.py) uses ordinary shuffled batches unless it is positive. This identifies a reproduction mismatch, not proof of what the published jobs actually ran. The released loop evaluates test metrics each epoch; the documented script selects by validation if a validation loader exists, but code silently falls back to test accuracy when it does not. A new experiment should require a nonempty validation loader, prevent test access until selection, and assert positive-pair coverage. Also, §280's inner-validation data used non-causal alignment windows, although the held-out person's test stream used causal alignment; that training-side selection mismatch is a potential limitation, not evidence of target-test leakage.

**Inference state audit.** The released [`GaussianInferenceLayer.forward`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wythedee/DEFT/main/CBraMod/disentangle_v7/model.py) initializes prior mean and precision as local variables on each call. Four layers iterate over tokens inside that call, with no adapter state retained for the next trial. The adapter therefore does not require previous target windows or a subject identifier. This is source inspection, not an end-to-end isolation test: the backbone, its mode, normalization and packaging must also be checked. Its released forward calls the classifier for both continuous and quantized logits even when the codebook is disabled and those tensors coincide; a simplified export needs numerical parity and timing checks. The [authors' repository](https://github.com/wythedee/DEFT) has no root-level license. `CBraMod/` and `LaBraM/` each include MIT notices from the original backbone authors; how those notices cover new DEFT contributions is unclear. Resolve rights before copying. A100 inference latency and whether distillation retains any benefit are unmeasured.

## E. Whole-system geometry alternatives

**HEEGNet — hyperbolic whole model.** [ICLR 2026 paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=CNDNRjpVIL), first preprint 6 January 2026: combines an EEGNet-like encoder, a hyperbolic classifier, and domain-specific moment then distribution alignment. It reports balanced accuracy 54.1% versus 54.6% for TSMNet with SPD domain-specific normalization on BNCI2014001, and 75.8% versus 74.3% on BNCI2015001. These are different MI datasets and domain-adaptation settings. The paper specifically identifies online hyperbolic normalization as future work. A session-local causal adaptation is therefore a new implementation, not a reproduction of the quoted numbers. [Code](https://github.com/fightlesliefigt/HEEGNet) is CC BY-NC 4.0 and its example uses float64. Evaluate a whole alternative system, with reset and prefix-only guards, not just one score of its unadapted base. Compare against matched TSMNet, since §288/§290 screened TSMNet only as an added base member, not as a complete alternative.

**LEAPNet — window-conditioned covariance reference.** The [publisher's 9 October 2026 abstract](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11732158/) describes learnable SPD anchors and a router that synthesizes a log-Euclidean reference for each trial; it claims no target-session aggregates. It reports a 24-session motor-execution study and a separate 51-person within-subject session holdout. This is plausibly compatible with a single delivered window, but the accessible source is an abstract only; we have not verified full equations, a reproducible code release, license or runtime. Inference: a trained per-window reference could be evaluated as a distinct model against our static/causal-reference covariance system, but its transfer to a new person and the three competition classes is unknown.

**CFSPMNet — state-space model.** [11 May 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10111): Fourier-conditioned state-space encoder plus physiological-prototype-gated pseudo-label adaptation. The authors report 68.23% and 73.33% on two held-out stroke-patient MI datasets. However, the method performs a second training stage using unlabeled target trials; its batch target adaptation must not be described as a valid online result for this challenge. Its 8–30 Hz filtering and binary left/right stroke-MI tasks also differ from our mixed cognitive classes. [Code](https://github.com/wxk1224/CFSPMNet) is present, with no visible root license. A strictly sequential, current-session-only adaptation or a target-free whole-model baseline would need fresh tests and rights clarification.

**MOPC — whole EEGNet ensemble with online adaptation.** The [2026 paper's released implementation](https://github.com/KunKun-Zhang/MOPC) uses current-session memory, perturbation consistency and calibrated pseudo-labels, optimizing BN affine parameters in one of five ensemble members. It reports +2.22/+3.67 points over its strongest baselines on two binary MI datasets and 40–60 ms per trial on a Xeon Silver 4210R CPU; neither quantity is a PROTEUS or A100 result. At source revision `3676810cc2d7fadfc91c6dfe69694e8f919291b7`, the BCI2b path creates a fresh model for each session, predicts before appending the trial, and updates using the previous memory bank. Its published evaluation is in recording order, so shuffled delivery and our three classes remain untested. The model returns probabilities but prediction and confidence loss apply softmax again, changing probability calibration; this behavior should be reproduced or changed only with a parity-controlled ablation. Although the optimizer contains only BN affine parameters, the update also invokes `model.train()` on ordinary batch-normalization layers: running means and variances change, and dropout becomes active. Session reset and parity checks must cover buffers and random-number state as well as weights. The original code is MIT, with separate notices for third-party parts.

## F. Negative controls and recent benchmarks

* [ICLR 2026: Are EEG Foundation Models Worth It?](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/f0f39b7686634fc81ca0112566b2c05f-Abstract-Conference.html) reports that fine-tuned EEG foundation models often fail to significantly beat compact neural or classical models in data-scarce settings; linear probes are consistently weak. This supports measuring both model families under the same task, not assuming either family wins. Local §71 and §280 already show weak frozen and mixed fine-tuning results for particular recipes.
* [EEG-FM-Bench, ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/xiong26f.html) finds that multitask training can regularize small datasets but can cause negative transfer, including on motor imagery. Pretraining exposure and task mismatch must be documented. Do not treat same-subject data under a new task name as independent held-out evidence.
* [Backbone-Informed Adaptation, 4 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07192): two EEG backbones and capacity-matched adapter controls show no consistent advantage for architecture-specific adapters. The PhysioNet-MI experiment lacks the matched-adapter pair, so it does not prove the result for motor imagery. Paired capacities, repeated seeds and subject-disjoint splits are necessary controls for DEFT or LEAF.
* [Channel Adaptation benchmark, 25 April 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23091): rigid input layouts need mapping, while interpolation or alignment may cause negative transfer when flexible encoders are fully fine-tuned. Probe and fine-tuned findings are not interchangeable. The authors used fixed model-wide learning rates and their own task protocols.
* [BrainPEC, NeurIPS 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11410): three EEG encoders, five **clinical** tasks and a 63-feature audit. Its selected features recover 79.3% of the measured model advantage over a same-dimension random-feature baseline on average. That is neither 79.3% of raw accuracy nor evidence that cognition is fully captured by these features. The [audit code](https://github.com/Kian-Chen/BrainPEC) is MIT licensed and can suggest a controlled feature/representation comparison.
* [Notaro et al., 11 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15204), Tübingen/DTU, distinguish identity removal from matching class geometry. Invertible congruence transformations, including covariance recentering, leave each subject's condition-distance matrix unchanged under the affine-invariant Riemannian metric. Their experiments also found recentering could **improve cross-subject decoding**: unchanged within-person geometry does not imply the decoder cannot benefit from repositioning people. The proof does not extend automatically to our log-Euclidean distances or arbitrary transforms. Reliable cross-person relational gaps could be estimated only in the 19-person, five-class cohort; our three classes and ten people offer far less information. The paper's batch estimates also do not establish streaming validity.
* [ScaleBrain, 10 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14035) tokenizes the same segment at multiple shared temporal anchors and uses matching scale-specific reconstruction. It reports 65.24% balanced accuracy on PhysioNet-MI against 64.80% for REVE, with 70 train, 19 validation and 20 test people; its main table combines results from corresponding published works when available, so exact comparability requires checking. Its TUEG pretraining includes TUAB/TUEV evaluation recordings, which the authors disclose. Five-seed PhysioMI model variability is ±1.11 points, so the 0.44-point difference is not by itself convincing. No official implementation or checkpoint was located in the paper. Local §277 did not try this exact objective, but pretraining on 9,797 hours is not an immediately reproducible short-deadline alternative.
* [Adeika and Osuagwu, 14 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20954) report that single-person-trained decoders for specific imagined-speech content transferred near chance over 482 ordered source/target person pairs across three corpora; none survived the authors' multiple-comparison correction. Their imagery-versus-pre-stimulus baseline generalized, but an additional time-control analysis suggested it captured a more general post-stimulus state. This challenges naive transfer claims from external speech corpora, consistent with our §278 WG-versus-rest mismatch. It does **not** show that our WORD-vs-SUB-vs-MI task is impossible, or establish an upper bound for multi-person training.

## Follow-up reading

[MAGE](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09928) uses 250 ms, 500 ms and 1 s tokens and a learned multi-scale aggregator; it claims 0.6M parameters and PhysioNet-MI results with a frozen encoder. The paper says code will be released, and no official implementation or checkpoint was located. Its arXiv page displays a 25 September submission date despite a `2611` identifier, so its exact chronology needs independent confirmation. The 19-channel clinical pretraining corpus, channel mapping, model selection and runtime need assessment before it becomes a concrete candidate. Dynamic weighting of within-window scales is distinct from §290 P2's manually selected temporal segments.
