## What changed our judgment

- **The historical replay needs repair.** Two Euclidean-alignment members retained cached recording-order features in otherwise shuffled delivery. For the example first delivery, the recorded alignment had used 94 earlier recording windows unavailable to the streaming model. This makes historical full-stack contrasts partial-delivery evidence. The audit measured the input mismatch and did not estimate its effect on accuracy. The staged ZIP streams inputs in the exact worker. The audit also fixed training-cache reuse across model and training options and rejected sample weights that the ordinary training path would ignore. No completed score has been tied to those training-cache defects. [Scoring audit](../../neural-interfaces-2026/evidence/next_wave_16nov/scoring_audit_18nov.md)
- **The useful speed gain is local to personalization.** Lazy covariance reproduced all **27,336** historical score vectors bit for bit and reduced measured CPU personalization time by **17% on held-new** and **80% on returning** streams. On identical live A100 inputs, both paired personalizers reproduced all **120** score vectors, using **79 instead of 120** covariance evaluations. Independent stock runs diverged at the first upstream neural refit. Whole-worker and sealed runtime gains remain unmeasured. [Historical profiling](cap250-c5-result.md) · [Exact-worker attribution](cap250-c5a-result.md)
- **Hardware changes prediction behavior as well as time.** Both exact frozen ZIPs ran on the RTX 2080 Ti proxy. V5.1b matched **960/960** A100 labels and cap250 **906/960**, with changes beginning at the first refit. Cap250's accuracy relative to v5.1b fell **0.729 pp** on those two reused Scherer people. Its warm time was **1.97–2.06×** v5.1b. The actual Codabench RTX model and sealed effect are unknown. [RTX proxy](rtx/RESULT_18NOV.md)
- **Real smoke tests caught two numerical issues before scoring.** F12's first A100 smoke caught a difference between single-window and batched outputs. Both arms passed at the original tolerance with FP32 convolution. F13's independent numerical check caught cancellation in FP32 weighted variance. The final replay used float64 moments in affected channels and preserved the original output on the first window. [F12 job receipt](wave/band-queue-18nov.md) · [F13 controls](fbc323/result-18nov.md)

## Was the day effective?

Yes for the sealed improvement, eliminating weak combinations, tightening validation and finding a stronger standalone direction. The leaderboard gain came from the staged cap250 archive. Today's newly tested variants did not qualify to ship. The main gain in model knowledge was that raw supervision improved the early prototypes, while the original causal FBCNet still set the stronger starting point. An oracle with true labels measured available error recovery. A live selector has to recover it from EEG alone.

The search covered many training and adaptation mechanisms. It was narrow in **where they entered the system** and **who tested them**. Most waveform integration tests used one fixed terminal blend. That is a small test of integration, and the affected historical replay simulated only part of the delivery order. P10 lost accuracy in all twelve early waveform integrations, a pattern an average alone obscures. The same public people drove many choices. Extra seeds and reorderings reduced fit and delivery uncertainty without adding new people. Many inexpensive experiments on a weak frontend also delayed comparison against the strongest historical standalone baseline.

The best next use of compute is to rebuild causal validation for both complete systems, retain the exact stronger FBCNet control from the first wave, and qualify changes in their actual ensemble slot. A new person cohort and a hardware-matched replay would answer more than another batch of near-identical terminal blends.
